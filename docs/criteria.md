Pulled from the full Participant Guide text you pasted on Oct 26. Everything, in the guide's own structure:
Problem statements (your project must build toward one; Stage One checks the fit)
Build for Your World: bring Meta's models into your domain, solve a problem you know firsthand.
Push the Frontier: use Meta's models on challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond.
Judging process, three stages
Stage One (pass/fail): does the project reasonably fit the theme, and did it reasonably use the Meta Model API and/or Meta's open-weight models during the hackathon. Fail here and nothing else is read.
Stage Two (scored): four criteria, equally weighted, each 0 to 10, with the guide's own descriptions:
Impact: "What's the real-world potential here? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit into one of the problem statements?"
Use of Meta AI Tooling: "How creatively did this team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised even us?"
Technical Depth & Execution: "Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does this feel like something that was thought through with real craft?"
Demo: "Is this a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch?"
Stage Three (finals): top 5 scoring projects per track advance; final judges watch the video demos live and rank them on the same rubric. Announced at the closing ceremony, Nov 9, 12 PM ET. Judging period overall is Nov 5 to 8.
Prizes, $1,000,000 total
Startups track ($550,000, your track): 1st $250K, 2nd $150K, 3rd $75K, 4th $50K, 5th $25K.
Indie Developer track ($400,000): 1st $150K, 2nd $100K, 3rd $75K, 4th $50K, 5th $25K.
Bonus prizes ($50,000):
Most Viral Project, $20,000: highest X engagement (likes, reposts, comments) through Nov 8 EOD; post the demo and submit the link through the platform.
Best Build on Meta Open Weight Models, $10,000: best project on Muse Glimmer or Muse Spark Open, self-hosted or partner inference; flagged in round one, verified by the infrastructure partner. (Not you: Director runs on the hosted API models.)
Community Vote, $10,000: most votes in the public gallery, one per verified account; winner announced Nov 16.
Best Agentic Use-Case, $5,000: strongest use of agents to complete tasks, make decisions, or coordinate multi-step workflows; judged on autonomy, effectiveness, and practical usefulness.
Best Creative Build, $5,000: most original creative project using Muse Image or Muse Wonder, judged on originality, execution, and how effectively the model is used in the final experience. (Quietly relevant to you: the sign repaint is a Muse Image build, and it's flagged during round one.)
Rules
Repositories must be public. Team size max 4; solo is fine.
Demo must only highlight what was built during the hackathon; judges must be able to clearly identify it. Failure to identify original contributions = immediate disqualification at the judges' discretion.
New work only: startups may build on existing products but must clearly identify what was newly developed.
Disqualification for policy violations or using code, data, or assets you don't have rights to.
Anti-project list (avoid): AI medical advice, basic RAG apps, simple image analyzers, simple AI-for-education chatbots, job screeners, nutrition coaches, personality analyzers, sports analyzers/coaches, anything political or war-related.
Submission mechanics
One submission per team/startup via the Cerebral Valley platform, by Nov 4, 9:00 PM ET; late submissions not considered. A named representative submits. Repo publicly accessible, video link accessible, everyone who worked on it listed. The demo video is a primary judging artifact alongside the code and the live product.
